Chapter 1

A coin in your favour

Somebody offers a bet on a bent coin that lands heads 60% of the times it is tossed. You bet on heads; win and your stake is doubled, lose and it is gone. You can bet as often as you like, any amount up to what you have.

Every bet is in your favour: on average a pound staked comes back as £1.20. The only question is how much to stake each time.

Try it. Pick a share of your money to bet and toss.

Chapter 2

Betting everything

Staking everything each time makes the average outcome as large as it can be. It also ends at the first tails, and you are left with nothing to bet with. The chance of ten heads in a row is 0.6 to the tenth power: 0.6%.

The average is huge only because of that rare run of luck. Almost everybody who follows this plan is broke within a few tosses.

Try it. Bet everything and see how long it lasts.

Chapter 3

The best share

Bet a fixed share of what you have instead, and the money grows by a steady rate per toss over the long run. That rate is highest at 20%: the chance of winning minus the chance of losing. This is the , worked out by John Kelly at Bell Labs in 1956.

At 20% the money grows by about 2% a toss on average and is typically 7.5 times bigger after 100 tosses. Bet twice that, 40%, and it typically shrinks, even though every bet is in your favour.

Try it. Move along the curve and see the growth rate.

Chapter 4

Twenty gamblers

Here are twenty people each starting with £100 and staking the same share. At 20% most finish well ahead, though the ride is bumpy. Many people bet less than Kelly's share, giving up some growth for a smoother ride.

In a 2016 experiment, 61 people were given $25 to bet on exactly this coin for half an hour. 28% of them went bust.

Try it. Change the share and toss for all twenty.

Sources and notes

The growth rate for a share f is 0.6 × ln(1 + f) + 0.4 × ln(1 − f) per toss, worked out in your browser. "Typically" means the median: half of gamblers do better and half worse. The twenty paths use random tosses, so they change each time.
