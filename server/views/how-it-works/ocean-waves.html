Chapter 1

The water stays put

Watch a gull on the sea: waves pass under it, but it bobs up and down and hardly moves along. A wave is a shape that travels; the water itself mostly goes round in circles and ends up where it started.

The circles shrink quickly with depth. Half a wavelength down they are only about a twentieth of the size at the surface, which is why submarines ride out storms by diving.

Try it. Follow one gold dot as the waves go by.

Chapter 2

Longer waves are faster

In deep water, a wave's speed depends on how far apart its crests are. Waves 10 m apart travel at about 4 m a second; waves 100 m apart, over 12. Four times the length, twice the speed.

So after a distant storm the long swells outrun the short chop and arrive first, which is why surfers watch the forecast for long, regular swell.

Try it. Race a long wave against one a quarter its length.

Chapter 3

Coming ashore

Once the water is shallow compared with the wave, the circles drag on the sea bed and the rule changes: every wave travels at a speed set by the depth alone, slower in shallower water.

The front of a wave slows first, so the crests bunch up and the same energy is squeezed into less water. The wave grows taller until it is too tall for the water beneath it, roughly when its height reaches four fifths of the depth, and it topples over: a breaker.

Try it. Watch the crests crowd together and steepen as the beach shelves.

Chapter 4

Tsunamis

An earthquake under the sea lifts the whole depth of the water at once, making a wave hundreds of kilometres long. Even the deep ocean is shallow for a wave like that, so it travels at the speed the depth sets: about 700 km an hour in water 4 km deep, as fast as an airliner.

Out at sea it may be less than a metre high and pass under ships unnoticed. Nearing land it slows and piles up exactly as a breaker does, but with far more water behind it. The tsunami from the 1960 Chilean earthquake crossed the Pacific and struck Japan about 22 hours later.

Try it. Change the depth and read off the speed.
