Chapter 1

The house edge

Blackjack is the one casino game where the odds change as the cards come out. A player who makes the mathematically best play every time (called ) still loses in the long run, but only slightly: with good rules, the house keeps under half a penny in every pound bet.

Casinos deal from a shoe of several decks shuffled together, six in this picture, and shuffle again when a plastic cut card comes out, about three-quarters of the way through.

Cards that have gone are gone. So what is left in the shoe is never quite a full deck's mix, and that is the whole of what counting uses.

Chapter 2

Keeping count

Counting doesn't mean remembering every card. The most common system, Hi-Lo, gives each card one of three values: 2 to 6 count +1, 7 to 9 count nothing, and tens, picture cards and aces count −1.

Add them up as they are dealt. That total is the running count. When it is high, more low cards have gone than usual, so the shoe is left rich in tens and aces.

Try it. Hide the count, deal twenty cards, keep the count in your head, then show it and see if you were right.

Chapter 3

The true count

A running count of +6 means more with one deck left than with five. So counters divide it by the number of decks still in the shoe, which gives the true count.

The bars show what the true count is measuring: how far the cards left in the shoe have drifted from a full shoe's share of low, middle and high cards.

Chapter 4

Why tens and aces help

A shoe rich in tens and aces is better for the player in several ways. Blackjacks come up more often, and a player's blackjack pays 3 to 2. A dealer showing a low card must keep drawing until they reach 17, and with more tens about they bust more often. Doubling down on 10 or 11 lands a ten more often.

Counters' rule of thumb is that each point of true count moves the edge by about half a percentage point. So at a true count of about +1, the player is roughly even, and above that, slightly ahead. It is a rough figure that varies with the rules and the system.

The counter's move is simple: bet small when the count is low and more when it is high.

Chapter 5

The catch

Played well, counting turns an edge of about half a percent against the player into about one percent for them. That is a tiny edge, and luck swamps it for a long time: a counter can lose for hours, or weeks, while doing everything right.

Casinos fight back by shuffling earlier, so good counts never arrive, and by asking players they think are counting to leave. Counting in your head is not illegal in the UK or the US, but a casino is a private business and doesn't have to deal you in.

Try it. Press Play and watch the true count wander. Most of the time it sits near zero.
