Chapter 1

A secret on a line

Suppose a vault's combination must never rest with one person, but any two of five trusted people should be able to open it together. In 1979 Adi Shamir found a neat way to do it with school geometry.

Write the secret as a point on the vertical axis of a graph. Draw any straight line through it. Then give each of the five people one different point on that line. Two points are enough to draw a straight line, so any two people can redraw it and read the secret where it crosses the axis.

Try it. Make a new secret. The line changes, and so does every share.

Chapter 2

One share says nothing

Somebody holding a single point has learned nothing useful. Infinitely many straight lines pass through one point, and they cross the axis at every height. The secret could be anything.

Add a second share and only one line fits.

Try it. With one share, see the lines it allows. Then hand in another.

Chapter 3

Any three of five

For a higher bar, use a curve instead of a line. A parabola needs three points to fix it, so with a parabola any three people can find the secret and two cannot. In general, a curve that needs k points gives a scheme in which any k people succeed and k − 1 learn nothing.

It doesn't matter which people come together, only how many. And more shares can be handed out later, just by giving out more points on the same curve.

Try it. Set how many shares are needed, then hand them in one at a time.

Chapter 4

Doing it properly

Drawn on ordinary graph paper, shares leak a little: they hint at how big the numbers are. So the real scheme counts round a clock instead, keeping only the remainder after dividing by a large prime number. The curve's points then look like noise, while the same sums still rebuild it from enough shares.

Here the clock has 31 hours. Shamir's scheme is used to guard master keys, so that no single person can use them, and losing a share or two doesn't lose the secret.

Try it. Make new secrets: the shares jump about with no pattern.

Sources and notes
