Chapter 1

Six from fifty-nine

In the UK's Lotto, you pick six numbers from 1 to 59, and six balls are drawn. Match all six and you win the jackpot.

How many different tickets are there? 59 choices for the first number, 58 for the second, down to 54 for the sixth, divided by the 720 orders the same six could come in. That makes 45,057,474. Only one of them wins.

Try it. Draw a few times and see how many of your numbers come up.

Chapter 2

How long is 45 million?

Buy one ticket every week, and on average you would expect to wait about 866,000 years for a jackpot. Modern humans have been around for about 300,000.

Buying ten tickets a week makes a win ten times as likely, which still means a typical wait of over 86,000 years.

Chapter 3

The smaller prizes

Matching some of the numbers is much likelier. Three matches come up about once in 96 draws; four, once in about 2,180; five, once in about 142,000.

Most draws match nothing or one number. Play a ticket against thousands of draws and the counts settle on these odds.

Try it. Play ten thousand draws against one ticket.

Chapter 4

Choose differently, share less

Every ticket is exactly as likely to win as any other. 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6 is as likely as any random-looking six. But people don't choose evenly: many pick birthdays, so numbers up to 31 are popular.

Only about 1.6% of possible tickets use numbers up to 31 alone, yet many players choose from them. If those numbers come up, the jackpot is likely to be split many ways. Choosing unpopular numbers can't improve your chance of winning, only your share if you do.
